Where will the fine particles be tomorrow, and the days after?

Every morning Otso takes the 00 UTC analysis of the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, the ECMWF weather forecast of the same hour and the fires seen by satellite, computes the transport of the air with FLEXGREEN and forecasts PM2.5 over all land, 12 hours to 5 days ahead, as an ensemble of 8 members. Otso is the coarse (0.4°) model of the Foehn paper, the one evaluated in Aurora's protocol, run on live inputs. The 1 km Foehn is not run in real time.

Research forecast. Otso is an experiment of a university group, not an official service. It has not yet been evaluated in real time, it is not monitored around the clock, and it does not replace the air-quality forecasts, indices and alerts of your national or local authorities. If you must decide something about your health, use those.

What Otso is, and is not

What it is

  • The coarse Foehn model of the paper, unchanged. Otso is Foehn-CAMS, the 0.4° version trained on the CAMS analyses of 2017–2022 in the protocol of Microsoft's Aurora, with the weights used for the paper's results. Nothing is retrained or tuned for the live run.
  • Live inputs, every morning. The 00 UTC CAMS analysis (13 species and columns), the 00 UTC ECMWF IFS forecast from the open-data service (winds, temperature, humidity, pressure, rain, to +192 h), and the fires of the previous day from GFAS. FLEXGREEN recomputes, for every land cell and every lead, where its air was.
  • A probabilistic forecast. Eight members per day. The map shows their mean, their 90th percentile and the share of members above 25 µg m⁻³; the city table and the NetCDF file also give the 10 % and 90 % quantiles.
  • Reproducible. Each day's inputs, their sources and the exact variables used are logged with the forecast (see the inputs field of index.json). The code is on the main branch of the Foehn repository, folder experiments/global1km/realtime.

What it is not, yet

  • Not the 1 km Foehn. The paper's kilometre-scale forecasts need the 1 km record that exists only for the past; Otso works at 0.4°, about 40 km.
  • Not evaluated in real time. The scores of the paper were measured on 2022 in a hindcast. The live chain differs in two ways we know of: the fire injection height comes from the GFAS variable injh, because the variable the model was trained with (mami) is no longer produced since GFAS v1.4.2 (January 2026); and the weather comes from the open-data IFS at 0.25° instead of the archived HRES. A hindcast over September 2026 is being run to measure the effect of both; its scores will be published here.
  • Not an operational service. It runs on a shared supercomputer (LUMI) when a GPU is free; a day can be late or missing. The last seven days are kept.
  • Not an alert. See the notice at the top. Otso is named after the bear of Finnish folklore: a thing of the forest, best observed from a distance.
