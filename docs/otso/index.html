Otso 1.2

betaupdated today

Where will the fine particles be tomorrow, and the days after?

Every morning Otso 1.2 takes the 00 UTC analysis of the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service, the ECMWF weather forecast of the same hour and the fires seen by satellite, computes the transport of the air with FLEXGREEN and forecasts PM2.5 over all land, 12 hours to 5 days ahead, as an ensemble of 8 members. Otso is the coarse (0.4°) model of the Foehn paper, the one evaluated in Aurora's protocol, fine-tuned on the live inputs of 2026. The 1 km Foehn is not run in real time.

Research forecast. Otso 1.2 is an experiment of a university group, not an official service. It has not yet been evaluated in real time, it is not monitored around the clock, and it does not replace the air-quality forecasts, indices and alerts of your national or local authorities. Put Otso on your phone

Scan the code with your phone to open Otso there, then add it to your home screen: Add Otso to your home screen: nothing to download from a store, it opens full screen like an app, keeps the last forecast when you are offline and updates itself every morning.

iPhone, iPad — Safari
  1. Scan the code, or open ammarkheder.com/Foehn/otso in Safari (not in another app's browser).
  2. Tap Share ⎙ (the square with an arrow, at the bottom).
  3. Scroll and choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.
  4. Otso is on your home screen and opens full screen; it keeps the last forecast when offline.
Android — Chrome
  1. Scan the code, or open ammarkheder.com/Foehn/otso in Chrome.
  2. Tap the Install Otso button below the code when it appears, or the ⋮ menu → Add to Home screen / Install app.
  3. Confirm. Otso appears with the other apps.
Computer — Chrome, Edge
  1. Open ammarkheder.com/Foehn/otso.
  2. Click the install icon at the right of the address bar, or menu → Install Otso (Firefox and Safari on Mac: keep it as a bookmark).
  3. Otso opens in its own window.

Your city is remembered on this device only (nothing is sent anywhere). A new forecast appears each day around 12:30 UTC. Research forecast, not an alert: the app sends no notifications.

The forecast

Hover or tap a place for its value; click to keep it and see how it evolves over the five days. Scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan. Fields: the mean of the 8 members, their 90th percentile (a plausible bad case), and the share of members above 25 µg m−3, the limit of the "moderate" class of the European Air Quality Index for PM2.5.

    Any city, town or village: 34,000 places above 15,000 inhabitants answer instantly (GeoNames, CC BY 4.0), the others come from OpenStreetMap as you type (© OpenStreetMap contributors). You can also click the map or use your position. The forecast is for the 0.4° cell (about 40 km) that contains the place.

      What Otso 1.2 is, and is not

      What it is

      • The coarse Foehn model of the paper, brought up to date. Otso 1.2 is Foehn-CAMS, the 0.4° version trained on the CAMS analyses of 2017–2022 in the protocol of Microsoft's Aurora, fine-tuned on the live inputs of January–August 2026, because the CAMS system of 2026 differs from the one it learnt from. Otso 1.1, the paper's weights unchanged, ran on 10 and 11 October 2026.
      • Live inputs, every morning. The 00 UTC CAMS analysis (13 species and columns), the 00 UTC ECMWF IFS forecast from the open-data service (winds, temperature, humidity, pressure, rain, to +192 h), and the fires of the previous day from GFAS. FLEXGREEN recomputes, for every land cell and every lead, where its air was.
      • A probabilistic forecast. Eight members per day. The map shows their mean, their 90th percentile and the share of members above 25 µg m⁻³; the city table and the NetCDF file also give the 10 % and 90 % quantiles.
      • Reproducible. Each day's inputs, their sources and the exact variables used are logged with the forecast (see the inputs field of index.json). The code is on the main branch of the Foehn repository, folder experiments/global1km/realtime.

      What it is not, yet

      • Not the 1 km Foehn. The paper's kilometre-scale forecasts need the 1 km record that exists only for the past; Otso works at 0.4°, about 40 km.
      • Weaker than in the paper's 2022 test, for now. The live chain differs from the paper in two ways we know of: the fire injection height comes from the GFAS variable injh, because the variable the model was trained with (mami) is no longer produced since GFAS v1.4.2 (January 2026); and the weather comes from the open-data IFS at 0.25° instead of the archived HRES. The scores on 1–20 September 2026 show a skilful forecast that nevertheless no longer beats the CAMS forecast; a fine-tuning on the 2025–2026 analyses is the next step.
      • Not an operational service. It runs on a shared supercomputer (LUMI) when a GPU is free; a day can be late or missing. The last seven days are kept.
      • Not an alert. See the notice at the top. Otso is named after the bear of Finnish folklore. Versions: 1.1, the paper's weights on live inputs; 1.2, fine-tuned on January–August 2026. A thing of the forest, best observed from a distance.
